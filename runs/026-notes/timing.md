# Timing check — slides/speaker-script.md

Method: counted approximate spoken (prose) words per slide in `slides/deck.md` — body text only;
code blocks and full citation blocks are shown on the slide, not read verbatim, so they are
counted only lightly or not at all (per the note in `talk-context.md` that citations appear on
the slide in full but the speaker paraphrases). Summed across all 53 slides in the deck (title
through close; the two "Sources" slides are not presented and are excluded).

- **Total spoken words: ~3,300**
- **Implied time at 140 words/minute: ~23 minutes 35 seconds (≈1,415 s)**
- **Content budget in `slides/outline.md`: 1,620 s (27:00), plus 180 s (3:00) Q&A, for a 30-minute
  total.**

## Does it fit?

Yes. ~1,415 s of implied reading time against a 1,620 s budget leaves about 205 s (~3.4 minutes)
of slack. That slack is not evenly spread: the diagram-heavy pattern slides (fan-out, pipeline,
writer-critic, parallel-workers — outline entries 7–10) read short against their per-slide
seconds because their budgeted time includes pointing at the diagram and pausing, not more
words. Conversely, several slides in segment C (the meta-example, entries 14, 16, 18, 19, 20)
run at or slightly over their individual per-slide budgets because they carry dense numbers
(dollar figures, turn counts, run numbers) that read slower than plain prose even at the same
word count. The two densest stretches — outline entry 19 ("Attempt two, step by step," five
slides) and entry 22 ("the rest of the evidence," three slides) — are exactly the first two
entries on the outline's own cuts list, which is the right place to trim if the speaker is
running long in the room; see `slides/speaker-script.md` for the full per-slide breakdown and
the three cuts in the order to apply them.

**Timing line:** ~3,300 spoken words ≈ 23m35s at 140 wpm, which fits the outline's 1,620 s
(27:00) content budget with about 3.5 minutes of slack.
